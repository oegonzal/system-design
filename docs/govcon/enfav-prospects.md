# Enfav LLC: federal demand and prospects

Measured on 2026-10-13 from USAspending, award data as of 2026-10-13, and from SAM.gov. This report
runs the search plan in [usa-spending-search.md](usa-spending-search.md), measures the comparison
that plan never makes, and checks each of its recommendations against the data.

Fiscal 2025 is the base year throughout. It is the last year the data holds whole, because the
Department of Defense publishes its awards 90 days after signing them. How each number was produced
is in [section 11](#11-method-and-caveats).

## 1. The answer

**The demand is real, it is large, and small firms win close to half of it. Getting in is the hard
part.**

- **It is one of the two largest things the government buys.** Computer systems design and related
  services, the industry group Enfav's three codes sit in, took $70.3B of contract dollars in
  fiscal 2025. That is 9.0% of all federal contracts, second only to aerospace manufacturing.
- **Your exact service is near the top of the whole list.** Application development support, the
  product service code for custom software work, was the sixth-largest of every product and service
  the government bought: $22.6B across 15,825 contracts.
- **Small firms take more here than anywhere else.** Small businesses won 46% of the group's
  dollars, $32.2B. Across all contracts the share is 23%. 18% of every small-business contract
  dollar in the government is in this one group.
- **It is growing again.** Fiscal 2025 dipped 4%. Fiscal 2026 is not whole yet, but civilian
  agencies finished it 11% up and Defense ran 21% ahead through June. That puts the year at $74B to
  $76B for the three codes, the highest of the five years measured.
- **Structure, not demand, is what stands in the way.** 90% of the dollars are ordered through
  contract vehicles Enfav does not hold. Half of the dollars written as set-asides need a
  certification Enfav does not have. 69% of the work is performed in Virginia, Maryland and the
  District of Columbia. 6,300 firms won a dollar last year.
- **Primes do hire firms of Enfav's size.** Under software prime contracts they report about 12,300
  first-tier subcontracts a year, and 85% of them are under $1M. Six integrators issue 60% of the
  ones in Enfav's size range.

### What this means for Enfav

Enfav holds an active SAM.gov registration and no certification, clearance or contract vehicle. That
standing reaches three lanes today.

1. **Subcontracting.** This is the widest lane. It needs no vehicle and no past federal prime award,
   and the primes that use it are few and known. Section 9 names them.
2. **Small stand-alone buys.** In fiscal 2025, 1,812 new contracts were awarded outside any vehicle
   to small firms with no certification required, roughly 35 a week across the whole government. 83%
   of them are under $250K.
3. **A vehicle.** The 90% of the money that is ordered through vehicles opens only with one. GSA's
   schedule normally asks for two years of corporate experience and offers a Startup Springboard
   track for younger firms.

The honest reading is a good market with a slow door. Nothing in the data says demand is the risk.
Three things in it are risks: the share that flows through vehicles, the crowd of registered firms,
and a pending rule that would let far larger firms compete as small. Section 6 covers that rule.

## 11. Method and caveats

**Sources.** USAspending's search API, read directly: `spending_over_time`, `spending_by_category`,
`spending_by_award` and `spending_by_award_count`, in prime and subaward mode. SAM.gov's entity API
for registrant counts. The app's own store for offers received.

**Volume.** 485 USAspending requests on 2026-10-13: 21 to confirm the filters and 464 for the
measurements, at no more than 24 a minute. One connection was dropped and the request answered on
retry. None was refused. 8 SAM.gov requests. None of these went through the app, so its request
ledger does not show them.

**Definitions.**

- **Contracts** are award types A, B, C and D: calls, purchase orders, orders and definitive
  contracts. Vehicles themselves carry almost no obligations and are excluded.
- **Dollars** are obligations by action date. They are not contract ceilings.
- **The three codes** are NAICS 541511, 541512 and 541519. **The industry group** is 5415, which
  adds 541513.
- **Small business** is the contracting officer's determination on each action. A few awards carry
  both, so the two shares add to slightly more than the whole.
- **New contracts** are those whose first action falls in the year. Their size is what has been
  obligated since.
- **A set-aside lane** is what the award record says. Orders under a vehicle often leave it blank.
- **Recipients** are recipient records with dollars in the year, found by paging the ranking to its
  end.

**Caveats.**

- **Fiscal 2026 is partial.** Defense and Army Corps of Engineers awards are published 90 days late.
  Every year-over-year figure here uses fiscal 2025, or splits Defense out.
- **Fiscal 2027 began on October 1** and holds two weeks of data.
- **Subcontract data is a floor.** See section 7.
- **Keywords match loosely** and descriptions are short. A keyword count is a floor.
- **The industry code on an order is the vehicle's**, not always the work's. That is why product
  service codes are used beside it.
- **The fiscal 2026 estimate** assumes Defense's last quarter is between last year's amount and this
  year's pace.
- **Names are as recorded.** One company can appear as several recipients.
