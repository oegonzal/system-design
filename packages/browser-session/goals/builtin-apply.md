# Mission

Apply to software engineering roles that can be worked remotely — never an internship — from Built In's job list: one application per listing, every form answered from the evidence recalled about the applicant or by the values its Answering policy names, and the operator asked only for what neither settles. A listing is taken whether Built In's own form takes the application or the employer's form on its own site does; a company applied to before, here or on another site that keeps the same record, is passed over. The session is signed in to Built In already. A job opens in a tab of its own and an employer's form in another: the session follows the newest tab.

## Unit of work

One application: from opening a listing's card to the confirmation that the application was sent, or the point at which the listing is set aside. Its label is the job title and the company as the job page names them.

## Marks

- "Dropbox": a cloud-storage upload helper beside a file control — never clicked; the résumé goes into the file control by upload. A mark is a word on a control, never a stop condition.
- "Google Drive": the same helper for another cloud store — never clicked.
- "Enter manually": a helper that replaces the file control with a text box — never clicked; the résumé is uploaded as a file.

## Unit identity

- company: the company as the job page names it, in the link above the title.

## Unit context

- landmark: main

## Advisors

- job-application

## Documents

- resume: the applicant's résumé, a PDF

## Taking a unit on record

- avoid unless "Go to Next Page": a company on record to avoid is taken only when no next page of results is listed — its taskStart declares `onRecordTaken: true`.

## The listing page

- The list is https://builtin.com/jobs/remote/hybrid?search=Software+Engineering&city=Los+Angeles&state=California&country=USA&allLocations=true, 25 cards to a page; the pages after it add `&page=2`, `&page=3` … and are listed below the cards as links "Go to page 1", "Go to Page 2", "Go to Page 3" …, beside the link "Go to Next Page" — an arrow with no text, listed on every page but the last.
- Each card holds a link naming the company, a link naming the job title — it opens the job page in a new tab — "Yesterday", "N Days Ago" or "Reposted …", "Remote" or "Remote or Hybrid", the places, pay and level, and sometimes a badge "Easy Apply": Built In's own form takes those, the rest apply on the employer's site. The company link, a heart that saves and a chevron that expands the card are never clicked. A card taken by Built In's own form shows "Applied" beside its title; the record under Units of work guards every company applied to.
- A cookie dialog in front of a page, on Built In or on an employer's site, is dismissed by its declining control — "Reject", "I do not accept", "Decline" — never by "Manage" or "Settings". One asking consent to a privacy notice before a form, as "Consent & Continue" does, is a required consent: agreed to.

## The job page

- Its address has /job/<slug>/<id>. A breadcrumb "<Company> > Jobs > <Title>" and a link naming the company sit above the title — links that open tabs of their own, never clicked; below are "Posted …", the place, pay and level, "The Role" — the description, under the main landmark — and a panel "Am I A Good Fit?".
- A bar fixed at the bottom offers how to apply, beside "Save", which is never clicked. For a job applied to on the employer's site, a link "Apply to job" whose address in the Snapshot carries `?handler=ApplyRedirect` opens the employer's own form in a new tab on another domain; a link of the same name whose address is /auth/login is the signed-out control and is never clicked. Clicking the link may first show, in this same page, Built In's own contact form — First Name, Last Name, Email, a résumé file control and a button "Continue", also named "Apply to job" — which step 4 answers before the employer's form opens. For an Easy Apply job, a link "Easy Apply to job" opens Built In's own form on a page of its own, in a new tab — its address has /apply/job/ — with the contact fields filled from the profile, required selects named only by a placeholder, each carrying its question as its context, a résumé control "Upload Resume" listed whether or not the page shows it, and the button "Submit Application". A page offering neither — the listing closed, or no apply control — is set aside. Once "Apply to job" is clicked, Built In may ask in a dialog on the job page whether you applied — "Yes, I applied", "Maybe later": nothing in it is clicked or answered, and the employer's tab, which opens on its own, is what the next step observes.

## The application form

- An employer's form is one page or a few: contact fields, a résumé file control — listed whether or not the page shows it, often behind a button "Attach" or "Upload", beside buttons "Dropbox", "Google Drive" or "Enter manually" that are never clicked — questions, equal-opportunity selects, consent checkboxes, and one button that sends it — "Submit application", "Submit" or "Apply". The page after it confirms: "Thank you for applying", "Your application has been submitted", or words to that effect. Before it, a form may ask for a code it emailed to the applicant — "We have emailed you a code to verify your identity", a code field and a button "Verify": the code is a field for the operator, who has the email, and "Verify" is clicked once it is answered. A page listing only its banner — "Sign In", a logo, a search — has not rendered yet: wait, once, for its first field, its apply control or its sign-in.
- A required question is marked "*", or named by the page after a refused advance.
- A form that demands an account before its fields — "Create Account" or "Sign In" where the fields should be — is an account wall. Workday's, on a host under myworkdayjobs.com, always is: set it aside on arrival.
- Built In's own form is one page. Its résumé control takes the document resume by upload even when the page names a résumé attached from the profile — the application carries the résumé supplied to the run, never the profile's copy — and "Submit Application" sends it. Its box "Save this resume to my Built In Profile" is left as the page shows it: the résumé supplied to the run is the one the profile should hold.

## Per-unit procedure

1. The list. When the page is not the list — the browser opened elsewhere, or this tab shows a job page left by step 3 — navigate to the list's address, with `&page=N` when the page the card was taken from is known, and wait for the cards. The list is paged, never searched.
2. Choose. On the page in view, choose among the cards whose title names a role this mission applies to and that are not the title and company of a unit listed under Units of work, nor at the company of a unit closed this session or of a company on record to avoid or as forbidden: the first of them, top down, badged "Easy Apply" — Built In's own form takes it — else the first of them without the badge. Click its title link — the plan's last action: the job page opens in a new tab — and say in the assessment which card and why. When every card in view that would otherwise qualify is at such a company, page forward by step 9; only when no next page is listed may a card at a company on record to avoid be taken, its taskStart declaring `onRecordTaken: true`.
3. Open the unit. On the step after the click, with the job page in front: taskStart, then, in the same plan, by what the page offers — the link "Apply to job" to the employer's site: click it, the plan's last action; the employer's form opens in a new tab, or Built In's contact form shows first and is answered by step 4. The link "Easy Apply to job": click it, the plan's last action; Built In's own form opens in a new tab and step 4 answers it on the next step. Neither: taskDone `skipped`. A job page whose company is listed under Units of work as forbidden gets no taskStart, and a taskStart the session refused — the company is on record; the refusal is listed under Objections to answer — opens no unit: in either case navigate this tab to the list's address, with `&page=N` for the page the card was taken from, and go on by step 2 — the next unit's close removes this tab, and the list behind it is in front again at the page it was left on.
4. Each form page. One askFields for every required field still empty, every field marked invalid — and with it every control beside it that holds nothing and makes up its answer: a country or a dialing code beside a phone number, a month or a year beside a date — and every field holding what is not the kind of answer it asks for — words where it asks for a link, an address, a number or a date — never fill a fact about the applicant yourself; any other field holding a value is kept. A résumé file control holding no file — listed whether or not the page shows it — takes the document `resume` by upload, and is never filled, clicked or asked for. A required consent — a checkbox, or a select whose option is "I agree" — agreeing to the terms, the privacy notice or the processing of the applicant's data — is not asked: click the box to tick it, select the option. Then, in the same plan, click the page's advance button — "Continue", "Next", "Submit application", "Submit" or "Apply" — as its last action. A button is clicked; no key is pressed for it.
5. Account wall or bot check. A form that demands an account or a sign-in before its fields: taskDone `skipped` at once. So does an employer's page that shows a bot check — a CAPTCHA, a picture or puzzle to solve, a "Verify you are human" box, or words saying a verification stops the sending — before its form or after its sending button ran: it is never answered, and the unit is set aside at once, naming it.
6. Refused. If the page will not advance, ask for the fields it marks and click its advance button once more. Refused again: taskDone `failed`. A required document other than the résumé — a cover letter or another file, with no field to type into — is never uploaded and is not a field to ask for: on the page that requires it, taskDone `failed` at once.
7. Confirmation. On the page right after this unit's sending button ran, when it says the application was sent: taskDone `done`. When that page says instead that the application is being sent — "Submitting", "Please wait" — the plan waits for the words that confirm it and ends there, with `done`: the taskDone comes on the next step, from the page as observed, never in the same plan as a wait.
8. After a close. The session closes every tab this unit opened; the next step observes the list at the page it was left on, and the next card is chosen by step 2.
9. Next page. When no card in view qualifies — each names a role this mission does not apply to, is a unit listed under Units of work, or is at a company on record — scroll down to the links below the cards and click "Go to Next Page". With no next page listed, a card at a company on record to avoid qualifies again by step 2, its taskStart declaring `onRecordTaken: true`; with none of those either, the list has no more results.

## Answering policy

Answer about the applicant from the evidence listed in the goal, citing it, or by a line of this policy that names the value a question takes; a field neither settles goes to the operator. A field that already holds a value — contact details the site filled in, a résumé already attached on an employer's form — is kept, unless the page refused it or it holds what is not the kind of answer the field asks for — words where it asks for a link, an address, a number or a date — which is answered from the evidence as an empty field is. A field with options takes one listed option verbatim. A number field, and any question asking how many, takes a bare whole number. A figure the page refused — marked invalid once it was typed — is retyped as bare digits: no currency sign, separator, unit or word, so a page that validates a number accepts it. A consent or an acknowledgment the form asks for — to the terms, a privacy notice, the processing or sharing of the applicant's data, a condition of the role or of its hiring process — is agreed to: a box ticked, a yes-or-no answered "Yes", the option that agrees or acknowledges chosen. A box asking the applicant to sign the application, or to certify that it is true, by typing their name takes the applicant's full legal name as the record states it. A question whether the applicant accepts, or is comfortable with, what the role or its hiring process asks — travel, relocation, where and when the work is done, how interviews are held, recorded, transcribed or noted, a background check or a screening, being put forward by an agency or a recruiter — takes the answer on record for it, else "Yes" or the option that accepts. A question whether the applicant can perform the role's essential functions with or without accommodation, is at least 18, or can obtain and keep a clearance or a suitability determination the role requires takes the answer on record for it, else "Yes". A question of work authorization or sponsorship is answered from the citizenship and authorization on record: a citizen of the country asked about — named, or as the country of the address on record — is authorized to work there and needs no sponsorship, now or in the future. The résumé is the one file ever uploaded, as the document resume into a file control by the procedure, never typed; a required upload of any other file is set aside by the procedure's refused-page step. A field the page hides is left blank; a file control is the procedure's to upload into, shown or not. A field that asks whether you are a bot, or any other question a person would find odd, is left blank; a code the site emailed to the applicant to verify their identity is no such question: it goes to the operator, who has the email, and is never left blank. An answer the operator gave before to a question about the applicant — work authorization, sponsorship, years with a skill, an account of past work — is evidence for the same question on every application however it is worded, and the latest answer stands; a question whether the applicant works or worked for, contracted for, was referred to, or knows anyone or has relatives at the employer applied to, or at any other organization, is settled by the standing answers on record and the record of employers, never by an answer given about another employer, and is answered "No" where they name that organization nowhere — the applicant has worked only for the employers on record, and a personal project is no employer; a figure — years with a skill, a count — is computed only from dates and figures on record, never from a skill named without them. A figure asked for several skills or technologies together is the smallest figure on record among those of them that have one — the years they were used together can be no more than the years of the one used least — and one of them with no figure on record leaves that figure standing. A figure asked for any one of several skills or technologies — "A, B or C" — is the largest figure on record among those named. A figure on record for a skill, a technology or a category — a standing answer, or the span of the dated roles that used it — is also the figure for a narrower form of it, a use of it or a skill that runs on it — a language within a platform, a framework on a runtime, one application of a field — where the record states no figure for the narrower one itself. A question asking how you heard about the opening, the role or the company is answered "Built In" where that option is offered, else "Other" or the option for a source the list does not name, else the first listed option naming a job board or job site, and a box asking which source is answered "Built In": every opening here is found on Built In. A threshold — at least N years, more than N, N+ — is answered from the figure or span on record for that very skill or category, yes where it meets it and no where it falls short; the dated roles on record that used it, summed, show at least that many years — enough for a yes they meet, never a figure or a no. A yes-or-no question that asks about several skills, tools, practices or conditions at once takes "Yes" when the record shows experience with any one of them or with the kind of work they belong to, and "No" only when the record shows none of either. A free-text answer about the applicant's experience is written as the applicant would write it to a hiring team: concrete with the systems, technologies, scale and figures on record, as long as the question deserves and the field allows — two to four short paragraphs for an account, one for a yes-or-no with its reason — never padded and never generic, showing fit through what the record holds and never asserting what the role needs. An employment-history section that names no employer lists the positions on record most recent first, each later section the next, every field of a section from its one position; a section that cannot describe a position still held — one asking a reason for leaving, or an end date it gives no way to leave open or mark current — passes over a position still held, and a position the record says is still held is marked current where the section allows. An equal-opportunity question — gender, race or ethnicity, veteran status, disability — takes the answer on record for that very question, else the option that declines to answer; an answer to another question never settles it. No line of this policy names a value for a name, a contact detail, an address, a date, an identifier, an answer about criminal history, nor any equal-opportunity answer but declining: those come from the record or the operator.

## Suggesting policy

Where the record answers a question, lead with record suggestions. Where it is silent on a question about the applicant's experience, offer a transferable draft when adjacent work is recorded, and an invented draft modelled on the applicant's past. A fact the record lacks — a name, a date, a figure, an employer, a contact — gets no invented draft. A recorded boundary against filling gaps in the record binds record and transferable suggestions and every value given from the record without the operator — never a value a line of the Answering policy names; it does not withhold an invented draft, which is offered for the operator to accept or refuse. A boundary against a particular claim — work authorization, a credential, a present employer — binds every suggestion. A draft is written as the applicant would write it to a hiring team: concrete with the systems, technologies, scale and figures on record, drawing on the stories on record whole and in the first person, as long as the question deserves and the field allows — two to four short paragraphs for an account of experience — never padded and never generic, showing fit through what the record holds and never asserting what the role needs.

## Stop conditions

- login-wall (stopped) "Create Free Account": Built In's "Sign up now" dialog, with "Create Free Account" and "Log In": the session has been signed out. An employer's account wall is no stop — step 5 sets it aside.
- blocked (stopped) "Verify you are human": a bot check standing where Built In's own page should be; it is never answered. An employer's bot check is no stop — step 5 sets it aside.
- mission-complete (complete): no "Go to Next Page" link is listed and no card in view qualifies.

## Never do

- Never type into a field the page hides or that asks whether you are a bot.
- Never sign in or create an account, on Built In or on an employer's site; never change the password, the profile or account settings.
- Never click a button that sends an application with a required field left blank or with no unit of work open.
- Never upload any file but the document resume, and never into anything but a file control.
- Never click "Save", a card's heart or a company's link, and never follow, share or message; never close or switch a tab, and never navigate a tab while a unit of work is open.

## Task events

- taskStart on the step after a card's title link is clicked, when the job page is in front, labelled "<job title> at <company>", with the page's URL and the fact "company" as the link above the title names the company. A card passed over unopened is no unit: no taskStart, no taskDone; nor is a job page step 3 leaves — at a forbidden company, or after a taskStart the session refused.
- taskDone `done` once this unit's sending button ran and the page says the application was sent; `skipped` when the job page is set aside before any field is answered — no apply control, the listing closed, an account wall; `failed` when a form was started and could not be sent after one retry, or requires a document other than the résumé; never `already-done` — a company applied to before is on record and passed over unopened. A taskDone is the plan's last action: the session then closes the tabs the unit opened, and the next step observes the list.
- terminate with the stop condition's cause word as soon as its page is seen; for a cause the Stop conditions tie to words, only on a page that shows those words.
