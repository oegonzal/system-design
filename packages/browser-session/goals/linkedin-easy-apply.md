# Mission

Apply, through LinkedIn's Easy Apply, to software engineering roles that can be worked remotely — never an internship: one application per listing, every form answered from the evidence recalled about the applicant or by the values its Answering policy names, and the operator asked only for what neither settles. The session is signed in already; it never signs in itself. Stay focused only on job items that have an "Easy Apply" option. Skip ones that do not, or that have already been applied to - this list item will have an "Applied" indication text on it to indicate it has been applied. If there are no more options on the list that can be applied to then go to the next page in the paginated list of https://www.linkedin.com/jobs/search-results/?{queryParams} - Do not redirect out of this page, it will never be necessary since "Easy Apply" items will open a modal dialogue in-page. Redirecting out of the page will cause the task to exit from the correct application setup and if you do exit then go back to the route https://www.linkedin.com/jobs/search-results/?{queryParams}. On the results page you should click the first card isn't in "Applied" status and is "Easy Apply" to prevent viewing cards that will not be applied to.

## Unit of work

One application: from opening a listing's card to the confirmation that the application was sent, or the point at which the listing is set aside. Its label is the job title and the company as the listing names them.

## Marks

- "Applied ·": applied to before — the card is passed over, unopened, with no unit. A mark is a word on a card, never a stop condition.

## Unit identity

- company: the company as the listing's pane names it, beside the title.

## Unit context

- landmark: main

## Advisors

- job-application

## Taking a unit on record

- avoid unless "Next": a company on record to avoid is taken only when no next page of results is listed — its taskStart declares `onRecordTaken: true`.

## The results page

- The search box is the textbox "Describe the job you want"; it shows the query the results are for. The banner's "I’m looking for..." box is a different control and stays empty. Before any search the page lists suggested searches as links — "Remote senior software engineer easy apply", "Internships tackling climate issues" and the like — which are other searches, not job cards.
- The results are a list of about 25 cards to a page. Each card is one button whose name is the card's whole text — job title, company, place, sometimes pay and badges, "Posted …" — ending in "Easy Apply" when the listing takes an Easy Apply application. A card applied to before carries "Applied ·" just before "Posted". Each card also holds a "Dismiss <job title> job" control, which hides the listing.
- Clicking a card shows its listing in the pane beside the list: the title, the company, and how to apply — a button "Easy Apply" (its visible text reads "Easy Apply"); or, for a listing applied to before, a heading "Application status" over "Application submitted" and its age; or a link "Apply on company website"; or the words "No longer accepting applications".
- Below the list, buttons "Page 1", "Page 2", … and "Next" page through the results, out of view until the list is scrolled down. The application form's advance button is also named "Next"; that one is inside the dialog.
- A dialog headed "Messaging" may cover the list once a page has loaded; Escape closes it. After a paging click, press Escape in the same plan, so the next observation reads the new page's cards.

## The application form

- "Easy Apply" opens the listing's own page (its address has /jobs/view/) with a dialog over it headed "Apply to <Company>", a progress bar and the page count ("1/6"). While the dialog is open the page behind it cannot be reached and the unit is in progress: the Easy Apply button is behind the dialog.
- Each page of the form ends in one button that advances it — "Next", then "Review", then "Submit application" on the last; "Back" returns a page and "Dismiss" closes the dialog.
- A text box whose counter reads "of 20 characters" is a number box: it takes a bare whole number only — a figure as digits, a period as its length in days, none as 0 — and shows "Invalid input" under anything else; boxes counting 127, 150, 200 or 500 characters take words.
- Contact info comes filled in — first and last name, email address, phone country code; "Mobile phone number" may be empty. The résumés uploaded before are radio buttons with one selected, beside an "Upload resume" button.
- A question the form requires is marked "*", or shows "This field is required" under it after a refused advance. Some forms ask, as a "Yes"/"No" radio question, whether the applicant agrees to the site's additional job application terms.
- The last page may show a ticked checkbox "Follow <Company> to stay up to date with their page" beside "Submit application"; some forms have none.
- After "Submit application" a dialog headed "Next best action" says "Your application was sent to <Company>!", with "Not now", "Update profile" and "Dismiss". It does not block the page, and the pane beside it then shows "Application status" over "Application submitted" and how long ago ("now"). The dialog is dismissed with "Not now" (or "Dismiss"), never "Update profile"; then the list is where the next job not yet applied to is found.
- "Dismiss" before submitting asks "Save this application?" with "Discard" and "Save".

## Per-unit procedure

1. Search. When the page is not the results of `easy apply software engineer remote` — the bare search page, another query's results, or a listing's own page after its unit closed — navigate to https://www.linkedin.com/jobs/search-results/?keywords=easy%20apply%20software%20engineer%20remote and wait for the list; if the textbox "Describe the job you want" then does not hold the query, fill it with exactly that text and press Enter. Suggested searches are never clicked. Once the list is the query's, it is paged, never searched again.
2. Choose. On the page in view, open the first card, top down, whose title names a role this mission applies to, whose name ends in "Easy Apply", that is not marked "Applied ·", that is not the title and company of a unit listed under Units of work, and that is not at the company of the open unit, of a unit closed this session, or of a company listed under Units of work as on record to avoid: click it, and say in the assessment which card and why. When every card in view that would otherwise qualify is at such a company, page forward by step 8; only when no next page is listed may a card at a company on record to avoid be taken, its taskStart declaring `onRecordTaken: true`. A card whose name does not end in "Easy Apply", or whose title names a role this mission does not apply to, is passed over unopened, with no unit; a card's "Dismiss … job" control is never clicked. A card whose listing the pane already shows is not clicked again — go on to step 3.
3. Open the unit. On the step after the click, with the listing in the pane and no dialog open: taskStart, then, in the same plan, by what the pane offers — "Easy Apply": click it; "Application status": taskDone `already-done`; only "Apply on company website" or "No longer accepting applications": taskDone `skipped`. After either close, the next card by step 2. A listing whose company is listed under Units of work as on record as forbidden — in progress in another session — is set aside without a unit: no taskStart, and the next card by step 2.
4. Each form page. One askFields for every required field still empty and every field marked invalid — and with it every control beside it that holds nothing and makes up its answer: a country or a dialing code beside a phone number — never fill a fact about the applicant yourself; a field holding a value is left as it is — unless it holds what is not the kind of answer it asks for, words where it asks for a link, an address, a number or a date, which the askFields takes too — and so is the selected résumé; "Upload resume" is never clicked. A question asking to agree to the site's additional job application terms is not asked: choose its "Yes". A required consent checkbox — agreeing to terms or to the processing of the applicant's data — is not asked either: click it to tick it. Then, in the same plan, click the page's advance button — "Next", "Review" or "Submit application". A button is clicked; no key is pressed for it.
5. Submit. On the page with "Submit application": untick "Follow <Company> to stay up to date with their page" when it is ticked, then click "Submit application".
6. Refused. If the page will not advance, ask for the fields it marks and click its advance button once more. Refused again: taskDone `failed`, click "Dismiss", then "Discard", and return to the list by step 1. A required document the page offers only as an upload button — a cover letter or another file, with no field to type into — is never uploaded here and is not a field to ask for: on the page that requires it, taskDone `failed` at once, click "Dismiss", then "Discard", and return to the list by step 1.
7. Confirmation. On the page right after this unit's "Submit application" ran — the dialog saying "Your application was sent to <Company>!", or the pane's "Application submitted": taskDone `done`, then click "Not now", and the next card by step 2.
8. Next page. When no card in view qualifies — each is marked "Applied ·", lacks "Easy Apply", names a role this mission does not apply to, is a unit listed under Units of work, or is at a company on record to avoid — scroll down to the buttons below the list and click "Next". With no next page listed, a card at a company on record to avoid qualifies again by step 2, its taskStart declaring `onRecordTaken: true`; with none of those either, the search has no more results.

## Answering policy

Answer about the applicant from the evidence listed in the goal, citing it, or by a line of this policy that names the value a question takes; a field neither settles goes to the operator. A field that already holds a value — the contact details, the selected résumé — is kept, unless the page refused it or it holds what is not the kind of answer the field asks for — words where it asks for a link, an address, a number or a date — which is answered from the evidence as an empty field is. A field with options takes one listed option verbatim. A number field, and any question asking how many, takes a bare whole number. A figure the page refused — marked invalid once it was typed — is retyped as bare digits: no currency sign, separator, unit or word, so a page that validates a number accepts it. Words the page refused the same way were typed into a number field: they are retyped as the bare whole number they stated — a period as its length in days, a quantity as its count — and as 0 where they stated none. A question asking to agree to the site's additional job application terms is answered "Yes". A consent or an acknowledgment the form asks for — to the terms, a privacy notice, the processing or sharing of the applicant's data, a condition of the role or of its hiring process — is agreed to: a box ticked, a yes-or-no answered "Yes", the option that agrees or acknowledges chosen. A box asking the applicant to sign the application, or to certify that it is true, by typing their name takes the applicant's full legal name as the record states it. A question whether the applicant accepts, or is comfortable with, what the role or its hiring process asks — travel, relocation, where and when the work is done, how interviews are held, recorded, transcribed or noted, a background check or a screening, being put forward by an agency or a recruiter — takes the answer on record for it, else "Yes" or the option that accepts. A question whether the applicant can perform the role's essential functions with or without accommodation, is at least 18, or can obtain and keep a clearance or a suitability determination the role requires takes the answer on record for it, else "Yes". A question of work authorization or sponsorship is answered from the citizenship and authorization on record: a citizen of the country asked about — named, or as the country of the address on record — is authorized to work there and needs no sponsorship, now or in the future. A résumé is never uploaded, nor any other file; a required upload is set aside by the procedure's refused-page step. A field the page hides is left blank. A field that asks whether you are a bot, or any other question a person would find odd, is left blank. An answer the operator gave before to a question about the applicant — work authorization, sponsorship, years with a skill, an account of past work — is evidence for the same question on every application however it is worded, and the latest answer stands; a question whether the applicant works or worked for, contracted for, was referred to, or knows anyone or has relatives at the employer applied to, or at any other organization, is settled by the standing answers on record and the record of employers, never by an answer given about another employer, and is answered "No" where they name that organization nowhere — the applicant has worked only for the employers on record, and a personal project is no employer; a figure — years with a skill, a count — is computed only from dates and figures on record, never from a skill named without them. A figure asked for several skills or technologies together is the smallest figure on record among those of them that have one — the years they were used together can be no more than the years of the one used least — and one of them with no figure on record leaves that figure standing. A figure asked for any one of several skills or technologies — "A, B or C" — is the largest figure on record among those named. A figure on record for a skill, a technology or a category — a standing answer, or the span of the dated roles that used it — is also the figure for a narrower form of it, a use of it or a skill that runs on it — a language within a platform, a framework on a runtime, one application of a field — where the record states no figure for the narrower one itself. A question asking how you heard about the opening, the role or the company is answered "LinkedIn" where that option is offered, else "Other" or the option for a source the list does not name, else the first listed option naming a job board or job site, and a box asking which source is answered "LinkedIn": every opening here is found on LinkedIn. A threshold — at least N years, more than N, N+ — is answered from the figure or span on record for that very skill or category, yes where it meets it and no where it falls short; the dated roles on record that used it, summed, show at least that many years — enough for a yes they meet, never a figure or a no. A yes-or-no question that asks about several skills, tools, practices or conditions at once takes "Yes" when the record shows experience with any one of them or with the kind of work they belong to, and "No" only when the record shows none of either. A free-text answer about the applicant's experience is written as the applicant would write it to a hiring team: concrete with the systems, technologies, scale and figures on record, as long as the question deserves and the field allows — two to four short paragraphs for an account, one for a yes-or-no with its reason — never padded and never generic, showing fit through what the record holds and never asserting what the role needs. An employment-history section that names no employer lists the positions on record most recent first, each later section the next, every field of a section from its one position; a section that cannot describe a position still held — one asking a reason for leaving, or an end date it gives no way to leave open or mark current — passes over a position still held, and a position the record says is still held is marked current where the section allows. An equal-opportunity question — gender, race or ethnicity, veteran status, disability — takes the answer on record for that very question, else the option that declines to answer; an answer to another question never settles it. No line of this policy names a value for a name, a contact detail, an address, a date, an identifier, an answer about criminal history, nor any equal-opportunity answer but declining: those come from the record or the operator.

## Suggesting policy

Where the record answers a question, lead with record suggestions. Where it is silent on a question about the applicant's experience, offer a transferable draft when adjacent work is recorded, and an invented draft modelled on the applicant's past. A fact the record lacks — a name, a date, a figure, an employer, a contact — gets no invented draft. A recorded boundary against filling gaps in the record binds record and transferable suggestions and every value given from the record without the operator — never a value a line of the Answering policy names; it does not withhold an invented draft, which is offered for the operator to accept or refuse. A boundary against a particular claim — work authorization, a credential, a present employer — binds every suggestion. A draft is written as the applicant would write it to a hiring team: concrete with the systems, technologies, scale and figures on record, drawing on the stories on record whole and in the first person, as long as the question deserves and the field allows — two to four short paragraphs for an account of experience — never padded and never generic, showing fit through what the record holds and never asserting what the role needs.

## Stop conditions

- limit-reached (complete) "limit daily submissions": the site says it limits daily submissions — today's Easy Apply applications are spent; a mark on a card is never a limit.
- limit-reached (complete) "daily limit": the site says a daily limit is reached.
- limit-reached (complete) "application limit": the site says an application limit is reached.
- limit-reached (complete) "Easy Apply limit": the site says today's Easy Apply limit is reached — measured 2026-10-20, its dialog reads "You reached today's Easy Apply limit" and names none of the phrases above.
- login-wall (stopped) "Sign in": a sign-in form, a verification challenge or a "confirm it is you" page appears.
- mission-complete (complete): no next page of results is listed and no card in view qualifies.

## Never do

- Never type into a field the page hides or that asks whether you are a bot.
- Never sign in, change the password, update the profile or touch account settings.
- Never click "Submit application" with a required field left blank or with no unit of work open.
- Never send a message to a recruiter, and never follow a company: the "Follow <Company>" box is unticked before "Submit application".
- Never apply to the same listing twice in a session.

## Task events

- taskStart on the step after a card is clicked, when the pane shows the listing, labelled "<job title> at <company>", with the page's URL and the fact "company" as the pane names the company beside the title. A card passed over unopened is no unit: no taskStart, no taskDone.
- taskDone `done` once this unit's "Submit application" ran and the page says the application was sent — the dialog, or the pane's "Application submitted" — never `already-done` for it; `already-done` only for a listing whose pane showed "Application status" when it was first opened; `skipped` when the opened listing is set aside before the form — no Easy Apply button, or the operator says /blank to every field of a page; `failed` when the form was started and could not be submitted after one retry.
- terminate with the stop condition's cause word as soon as its page is seen; for a cause the Stop conditions tie to words, only on a page that shows those words.
